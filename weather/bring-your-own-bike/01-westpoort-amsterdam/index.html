What the Weather Gave Us

The first edition of BYOB took place under a nationwide storm warning. The route ran from Hembrugterrein, a former munitions factory site in Zaandam that remained closed to the public for decades, across the North Sea Canal by ferry to Westpoort, Amsterdam’s industrial port.

Along the way, the ride passed wind turbines powering the harbour, scrap metal terminals loading compressed steel for export to Turkey, and the oil terminals of the world’s largest gasoline port. With a 35 mph headwind resisting every pedal stroke, weathering shifted from a concept to a physical condition, one the cyclists had to push through with their whole bodies. It traced how the militarised past and the fossil-fuelled present of the Amsterdam waterfront meet the body within the same weather.

BYOB (Bring Your Own Bike) is a participatory cycling performance series that explores polluted weather as a collective, embodied phenomenon. Participants pedal through polluted industrial areas on their own bikes, weathering together under the guidance of the artist.

A bike is not merely a medium here. It is a speculative tool that turns the body into a sensing instrument, tuned to headwind, exhaust, the smell of oil rising off water. Each stroke of the pedal is a small negotiation with the air itself, a communal practice at the intersection of environment, socio-political climate, and the human body.

The performance has taken place across industrial sites in Europe and South Korea, ports, refineries, energy infrastructures, each ride shaped by the uneven atmosphere and politics of its location: who breathes what, and where, and who is even allowed to get close enough to ask.
